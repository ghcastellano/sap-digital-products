CHECKPOINT 03 · WHAT KIND OF DEMAND

From 6+ overlapping taxonomies to four orthogonal dimensions.

Today's classification depends on which team you ask. Tomorrow, every card carries the same four-axis label, captured once at intake.

× AS-IS

Two dimensions · used inconsistently

Three official type buckets (Regulatory · Non-Regulatory · Production Support) mixed with team-specific size variants (Break/fix · Small · Medium · Large · Minor · Incidents). Each team uses its own combination.
  • GIS uses · Break/fix · Small · Large
  • Corporate uses · Regulatory · Small/Medium
  • G&E uses · Small · Medium · Large · BRP cycles (only mature)
  • Smart Metering uses · Incidents · Large · Small (intrinsic common sense)
→
✓ TO-BE

Four orthogonal dimensions · captured once

Every demand carries four labels at intake. Each label is a mandatory field on the Jira request type. Each dimension drives a different downstream behavior.
  • Class of Service — Standard · Fixed-Date · Expedite ≤20% · Intangible
  • Size (T-shirt) — S / M / L / XL (drives routing path)
  • Investment intent — Run / Grow / Transform
  • Work hierarchy — Initiative → Story (Epic only when > 3 months)
DIMENSION 01

Class of Service

  • Standard — pulled by capacity
  • Fixed-Date — locked sequence
  • Expedite — capped ≤ 20% throughput
  • Intangible — 10–15% capacity reserve
DIMENSION 02

Size · T-shirt

  • S · ≤ $10k · 1–3 months · < 5 res. → squad direct
  • M · ≤ $500k · 3–6 months → squad direct, flagged
  • L · cross-team → Portfolio Gate
  • XL · multi-quarter → Gate + VP Sync
DIMENSION 03

Investment intent

  • Run — operations · stability
  • Grow — enhancement · new features
  • Transform — re-platform · innovation
  • Mix per VS · target ratio set per quarter
DIMENSION 04

Work item hierarchy

  • Initiative — portfolio level (≤ 3 mo)
  • Story — squad level (default unit)
  • Epic — exception, only Initiative > 3 mo
  • Default flow · Initiative → Story

Capabilities

What the organization must be able to do, end to end.
01

Demand intake channels (formal and informal)

AS-IS6+ disconnected channels (Jira · IT Now · RITM · SharePoint · Email · Word). No formal versus informal distinction — everything is informal except for G&E.
TO-BEOne Jira request type per Value Stream as the only formal channel. IT Now restricted to incidents. Informal channels (email · Teams · Word) decommissioned for new demand.
02

Demand registration and logging

AS-ISPer-team spreadsheets, OneNote pages and email threads. No SLA, no volume tracking. Logging only for items that hit PDD threshold.
TO-BEAuto-created backlog item from intake form with immutable audit fields (requester · BU · team · date · sponsor). Every state change logged.
03

Channel consolidation level (single vs multiple entry points)

AS-ISMultiple entry points per team. Same OPCO can drop the same demand into 3+ different channels with no de-dup.
TO-BEOne adaptive intake form per Value Stream. Target — 90% of demand via the single channel by month 6. No alternative formal channels accepted.
04

Standard intake formats or templates

AS-ISNo standard template. Each team improvises — some use a Word doc, some use a Jira free-text card, some send PowerPoint slides.
TO-BECanonical template enforcing Definition of Ready — title · business value · acceptance criteria · Class of Service · due date · sponsor.
05

Demand classification (type, size, urgency)

AS-ISThree official buckets (Regulatory · Non-Regulatory · Production Support) mixed with per-team size variants. Inconsistent across teams.
TO-BEThree independent axes — type · size · urgency. Closed taxonomy. Classification done at the intake gate, never self-declared.
06

Run · Change · Regulatory · Innovation differentiation

AS-ISImplicit. Regulatory is the default priority by convention. Innovation appears only as a one-off project line in the Large-Demand portfolio.
TO-BEFour explicit Classes of Service with capacity caps. Replaces the ad-hoc "urgent override" culture with a numeric policy (Expedite ≤ 20% · Intangible 10–15%).
07

Business value definition

AS-IS"Intrinsic common sense" (Smart Metering's exact wording). PDD provides a value section but it's narrative, not scored. No shared definition.
TO-BEWeighted Pain Score combining Business Value · UX · Safety · Effort. Sponsor signs off the score at intake. CS-weighted for UX-heavy · IT-weighted for Safety-heavy.
08

Availability of acceptance criteria

AS-ISSometimes present in PDD documents for Large items. Almost never present for Small / break-fix demand — closure decided by the executor.
TO-BEMandatory in the Definition of Ready. Format · Given / When / Then or measurable outcome. Reviewed before scoring. Immutable post-prioritization.
09

Technical feasibility input

AS-ISAsked late, often after the demand is already approved. Tech leads pulled into ad-hoc reviews when items get stuck.
TO-BELightweight check at the intake gate by a rotating architect. Time-boxed (2 hours max). Output · Feasible · Conditional · Not feasible. Advisory, not a veto.
10

Triage and initial validation activities

AS-ISNo formal triage for items below PDD threshold. Sr Managers absorb intake reactively. SLA undefined.
TO-BEWeekly 30-minute Gate 1 triage committee. Outcomes · Accept · Send-back · Reject · Redirect. Five-business-day SLA. Decision logged with reason.
11

Demand routing and assignment

AS-ISRouting happens by tribal knowledge — the requester knows whom to email. Cross-team items bounce around for weeks.
TO-BERouting by Value Stream, not by team or person. Auto-routing based on the requester's selection. Cross-stream items go to Architecture review.
12

Handling of urgent or exception requests

AS-IS"Regulatory always wins" + "Incidents first → strategic starved" (Global SAP). Any sponsor with enough seniority can claim Expedite.
TO-BEExpedite Class of Service capped at 20% of throughput. Real-time incidents bypass intake entirely (Service Desk SLA). Every Expedite reviewed at the next Gate 2.
13

Demand visibility and tracking

AS-ISVisibility is monthly · manual · per-team · in PowerPoint. Leadership lacks real-time view of inflow, capacity, or status.
TO-BESingle dashboard, public read-only. Five views — inflow funnel · backlog by team · CoS mix · aging · top priorities. Auto-refresh under one hour.
14

Identification of duplicate or overlapping demand

AS-IS"PDD is the driver. Lots of overlapping projects" (Amit Patel quote). De-dup happens by accident when two PMs talk.
TO-BESimilarity search at submit (top-3 matches surfaced). Weekly de-dup query. Overlaps escalated to the Portfolio Committee.

Roles

Who owns and operates the framework.
01

Business requestors / stakeholders

AS-ISAnyone with a problem and an email address. No standard sponsor sign-off. Requesters often submit without business-value justification.
TO-BEAll originating business units route through a single intake form per Value Stream. Sponsor sign-off mandatory at intake. Status visible through requester portal.
02

Product or Demand Managers

AS-ISThe Product Manager role does not exist at Avangrid today. Project Managers (PJMs), Sr Managers and area Champions absorb the role inconsistently.
TO-BEOne Product Manager per Value Stream Team — accountable for outcome, scoring and backlog priority. NEW role for all 8 squads. Bridges IT to business (folds in Process Owner / Demand Manager scope).
03

Architects or technical reviewers

AS-ISNo standing architecture function. Technical reviews happen ad-hoc when something goes wrong. Solution and Integration Architect roles absent.
TO-BEArchitecture Council with rotating members from existing technical leads. Solution Architect (SAP) and Integration Architect roles owned by the Capability Crew. Time-boxed advisory at intake gate (≤ 2 h).
04

Triage / intake committee

AS-ISNo committee. No standard cadence. No published outcomes. Conflicts surface in 1:1 conversations between Sr Managers.
TO-BEWeekly committee per portfolio. Composition · Demand Manager (chair) plus PMs and Architect. Decisions logged with reason. Cross-portfolio escalations go to monthly Portfolio Committee.

5 ITEMS

Tools

The systems and platforms used end to end.
01

Demand capture tools

AS-ISEmail, Teams, Word, PowerPoint, OneNote, SharePoint, Jira free-text cards, IT Now tickets, BSS Log, PDD workbook — all parallel.
TO-BEOne Jira request type per Value Stream. Mandatory fields enforce DoR. Size captured as a field (S · M · L · XL), not a separate request type. IT Now reserved for incidents only.
02

Demand tracking and backlog tools

AS-ISPer-team Excel sheets and Jira boards. Smart Metering uses PPM Now. No common backlog view.
TO-BESingle Jira backlog system as system of record. Item hierarchy · Initiative → Story (Epic optional when Initiative > 3 mo). Run vs Build separated tracks per squad.
03

Communication channels

AS-ISStatus delivered by ad-hoc reply, sometimes by Teams chat, sometimes never. Requester chases the executor.
TO-BETriage outcomes auto-published to requesters. Per-requester portal with live status. Cross-team coordination through dedicated forums (5 forums, named cadences).
04

Documentation tools for capturing request details

AS-ISDocumentation lives in PDD (large items only). For everything else, the request body itself is the documentation. Inconsistent, often missing.
TO-BEConfluence as single source of truth for Definition of Ready, Class of Service taxonomy, per-team product brief. Sponsor approval logged within the intake form itself.
05

Reporting or visibility tools

AS-ISMonthly PowerPoint by Sr Managers. No real-time visibility. Director-level rollups manually built.
TO-BEDemand dashboard with five views — inflow funnel · backlog by team · class-of-service mix · aging · top priorities. Auto-refresh under one hour. Director-level rollups automatic.
04
6 ITEMS

Governance

Decisions, forums, and controls that keep the funnel honest.
01

Demand review forums

AS-ISNine recurring forums exist but none covers portfolio-level decisions. ERP Steering Committee · MISSING.
TO-BETwo-gate structure. Gate 1 — weekly triage (30 min). Gate 2 — monthly Portfolio Committee (90 min). Quarterly cross-portfolio Big Room Planning.
02

Decision-making practices for accepting, rejecting, or redirecting demand

AS-ISDecisions made in 1:1 conversations or absorbed silently. No published outcomes. No reasons logged.
TO-BEFour explicit outcomes — Accept · Send-back · Reject · Redirect. Every decision audit-logged with reason. No silent overrides.
03

Escalation mechanisms for urgent or conflicting requests

AS-ISEscalation by political weight. Whoever shouts loudest gets the slot. Conflicts surface late, in delivery.
TO-BECross-team conflicts → Customer Journey Forum. Cross-portfolio conflicts → VP Portfolio Sync. Every Expedite reviewed at the next Portfolio Committee.
04

Definition of intake guidelines or policies

AS-ISEach team has its own informal rules of thumb. No published guideline document. New requesters learn by trial and error.
TO-BESingle source of truth for the Definition of Ready template, Class of Service taxonomy, and capacity caps. Reviewed every six months by the Demand Council.
05

Alignment mechanisms with business stakeholders

AS-ISOne Customer Journey Forum exists for some streams; no consistent rhythm. Sponsor sign-off optional.
TO-BEQuarterly Big Room Planning. Customer Journey Forum per major customer journey. Sponsor sign-off mandatory at intake (business value approval).
06

Oversight of demand inflow and backlog health

AS-ISNo backlog-health metric. No aging policy. Items rot in queues for months without anyone noticing.
TO-BEDemand Manager owns the demand dashboard. Aging beyond threshold triggers automatic escalation. Quarterly demand-vs-delivery conversion review.

7 ITEMS

Performance

How success is measured across the demand funnel.
01

Demand volume and inflow trends

AS-ISNo volume data. Sr Managers estimate counts from memory. Forecast 30–50% growth without baseline.
TO-BESubmissions per week, month, quarter. Breakdown by Value Stream, Class of Service, OPCO, and source business area.
02

Demand quality (completeness, clarity)

AS-ISQuality is felt, not measured. "We need to have standardization — processes, demand intake and report" (Carlos Garbin quote).
TO-BEDefinition-of-Ready pass-rate at first submission (target 70% or higher). Re-submission rate per requester. Acceptance criteria quality score.
03

Triage lead time

AS-ISUntracked. Anecdotally weeks for Large items, days-or-never for Small. No SLA exists.
TO-BEMedian time from submission to Gate 1 decision (target ≤ 5 business days). 90th-percentile to surface outliers. Breakdown per Class of Service.
04

% of demand entering through formal channels

AS-ISUnknown — most demand never enters a system at all. Items live in email and Teams threads until someone surfaces them.
TO-BETarget 90% via single channel by month 6. Decommissioned-channel residuals tracked monthly. Per-business-unit adoption visible.
05

% of urgent or bypassed requests

AS-ISEffectively all regulatory items + many Sponsor-driven items skip the queue. Cap unknown · ratio unknown.
TO-BEExpedite as percent of total throughput (cap 20%). Regulatory subset tracked. Auto-downgrade rate of unauthorized "urgent" claims.
06

Demand duplication or overlap indicators

AS-ISDiscovered late, in delivery, when two teams build the same thing. Cost invisible.
TO-BEDuplicate-link rate (% submissions matched to existing). Overlap escalations resolved at Portfolio Committee.
07

Visibility and traceability of demand status

AS-ISRequester finds status by emailing the executor. No portal. No public read-only view. Status updates manual.
TO-BEPercent of requesters who can find own submission status (target 100%). Average time to status update. Live per-business-unit portal.
